Part 4: Pathways and biology

QUESTION
What biology do the genes that differ between type 2 diabetes (T2D) and non-diabetic (ND) islets point to, does it fit what is known about T2D, and does it depend on particular donors?

DATA
Input from Part 2: de_results.csv, the DESeq2 result for all 19,072 tested genes (design ~ batch + sex + group, T2D vs ND, 8 donors per group). No new sequence data was downloaded. Gene sets came from Enrichr (MSigDB Hallmark 2020, Reactome Pathways 2024, GO Biological Process 2025; provenance and checksums in GENESETS.md) and from the g:Profiler API (GO:BP, GO:MF, GO:CC, Reactome).

WHAT WE DID, WHY AND HOW
1. Ranked gene list (results/ranked_list.csv)
   Why: Part 2 found only 6 genes at FDR < 0.05. A list that short offers limited power for over-representation testing, so the main analysis had to use every gene rather than a cutoff.
   How: all genes with a gene symbol were ranked by the DESeq2 Wald statistic. Where two Ensembl genes shared a symbol the one with the larger absolute statistic was kept. This left 17,358 of the 19,072 tested genes; 1,687 had no symbol and 27 were duplicate symbols.
2. Gene set enrichment analysis (results/gsea_*.csv, figures/dotplot_gsea_*.png)
   Why: GSEA asks whether the members of a known pathway sit together at one end of the ranking, so it can detect many genes shifting slightly in the same direction, which is what 8 donors per group can realistically show.
   How: gseapy prerank, 10,000 permutations, sets of 15 to 500 genes, seed 7. GSEApy estimates FDR the standard GSEA way, from the observed and permutation NES distributions, not by Benjamini-Hochberg correction. Sets tested after the size filter: 50 of 50 Hallmark, 1,257 of 2,104 Reactome, 2,305 of 5,342 GO:BP. Preranked GSEA permutes gene labels rather than sample labels, because a ranked list carries no phenotype. The GSEA User Guide notes that gene-set permutation almost always returns more significant results than phenotype permutation, since it ignores correlation between genes in the same pathway, and that FDR < 0.05 rather than the usual FDR < 0.25 is the appropriate threshold in that mode. We used FDR < 0.05 throughout. The libraries also cover only part of our ranking: 68% of ranked genes appear somewhere in GO:BP and 51% in Reactome.
3. Over-representation analysis (results/ora_gprofiler.csv, figures/dotplot_ora_up.png)
   Why: a second method on the same data, to see whether a fixed gene list points the same way as the ranking. It is not independent evidence, because it uses the same donors and the same DESeq2 output.
   How: g:Profiler with the tested genes as a custom background and g:SCS correction, which is g:Profiler's own correction for the structure of the GO and Reactome graphs rather than Benjamini-Hochberg FDR. Two queries were submitted, both using an uncorrected nominal p < 0.01 cutoff on the DESeq2 result: 274 genes higher in T2D and 32 lower. The raw-p cutoff was used because an FDR-based list would have held 6 genes, which offers limited power for over-representation; the analysis is therefore labelled exploratory. g:Profiler recognised 270 of the 274 query genes and 18,909 of the 19,072 background genes.
4. Donor influence analysis (results/donor_influence.csv, figures/donor_influence.png)
   Why: Part 2 reports that P7_T2D and P8_T2D carry a strong hypoxia and stress signature and that dropping them removes all six significant genes. Losing significance in a smaller cohort can reflect reduced power; same-size exclusions provide a comparison but do not control all batch differences.
   How: DESeq2 was re-fitted 21 times: all 16 donors, each of the 16 single-donor exclusions, the P7_T2D + P8_T2D pair, and three other T2D pairs of the same size (n = 14, 6 T2D). For each fit a normalised enrichment score (NES) was computed for four fixed gene sets with an identical weighted Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic and 2,000 gene-label permutations, normalised against the same-sign permutation mean, together with the mean log2 fold change of the set members, so that effect size could be compared and not only whether FDR crossed 0.05. Three comparison pairs were tested, not all 28 possible T2D pairs.
5. Full-library sensitivity (results/hallmark_sensitivity.csv, results/library_wide_sensitivity.csv)
   How: GSEA was re-run on the 14-donor ranking for all three libraries with settings identical to the 16-donor run (1,000 permutations, seed 7).
6. Cell composition check
   How: Part 2's per-donor endocrine, acinar and ductal read fractions, which are marker-gene proxies rather than cell counts, were compared between groups, and the positions of INS, GCG, SST and the exocrine markers PRSS1, CPA1 and KRT19 in our ranking were recorded. This is a check for an obvious composition difference, not a measurement of beta-cell abundance.
7. Comparison with the source study (results/fadista_silenced_genes_check.csv)
   How: the four genes whose silencing reduced insulin secretion in INS1 cells in Fadista et al. 2014 were looked up in our results. PAK7 appears under its current HGNC symbol PAK5, which is what the Ensembl 110 annotation uses.

RESULTS
- Significant sets at FDR < 0.05: Hallmark 26 of 50, Reactome 125 of 1,257, GO:BP 173 of 2,305. g:Profiler returned 94 significant terms for the up list and none for the 32 down genes (both queries re-executed live on 26 November 2026: ora_gprofiler_rerun.csv and ora_gprofiler_down_rerun.csv), so the reduced direction rests on GSEA alone.
- Higher in T2D: Hallmark Hypoxia NES 2.72, TNF-alpha Signalling via NF-kB 2.71, Epithelial Mesenchymal Transition 2.64, Inflammatory Response 2.11, Glycolysis 1.98 (all FDR < 0.001); Reactome Collagen Formation 2.46; GO Extracellular Matrix Organization 2.35, Cellular Response to Hypoxia 2.29. g:Profiler agrees: response to hypoxia is its most significant term (adjusted p = 1.4e-12, 28 of 270 recognised genes), followed by vasculature development and angiogenesis.
- Lower in T2D: Hallmark Protein Secretion NES -2.13 and Pancreas Beta Cells NES -1.80 (nominal p 0.005, FDR 0.004); Reactome tRNA Processing in the Mitochondrion -3.12, rRNA Processing in the Mitochondrion -2.95.
- Donor influence. Dropping P7_T2D and P8_T2D moves Cellular Response to Hypoxia from NES 2.31 to 1.19 and its mean log2 fold change from 0.54 to 0.06, an 88% fall, while the three other tested T2D pairs of the same size leave it at 2.19 to 2.39 with mean log2 fold change 0.55 to 0.64. Because those pairs remove the same number of samples, the pattern is consistent with donor-specific influence beyond the loss of sample size, although only three comparison pairs were tested and all are from batch A, whereas P7/P8 are both from batch B.
  Extracellular Matrix Organization moves in the same direction but less strongly: NES 2.35 to 1.62, below all three comparison pairs (2.12 to 2.55), with mean log2 fold change falling 43% (0.600 to 0.340) against essentially no fall for the comparison pairs. Across the 16 single-donor exclusions, hypoxia drops as low as 1.85 while ECM never falls below 2.03. So the matrix signature is donor-influenced in the same direction as hypoxia, but the evidence for it is weaker, and we state it as such.
  Reactome mitochondrial tRNA processing behaves similarly (-3.13 to -1.95 against -2.89 to -3.15).
  Pancreas Beta Cells shows no sign of being driven by these donors. Its NES is negative in all 21 refits, ranging from -0.98 to -2.42; the P7/P8 value of -1.72 sits inside the range produced by the other 20 refits, and the four two-donor exclusions span only -1.56 to -1.72. The widest single-donor effect is the removal of P4_ND, which weakens it to -0.98, so the set is not insensitive to every donor; it is simply not driven by P7_T2D or P8_T2D.
- Full-library sensitivity. In the Hallmark library 14 of the 26 significant sets lose significance without P7/P8, 12 keep it and 15 others gain it. Hypoxia and Glycolysis lose it, falling to NES 0.89 (FDR 0.76) and -1.22 (FDR 0.12). TNF-alpha Signalling via NF-kB keeps it at NES 2.18, Epithelial Mesenchymal Transition at 2.30 and Inflammatory Response at 1.50 (FDR 0.031, the only borderline value among these). Pancreas Beta Cells keeps it with almost no change, NES -1.79 to -1.76 at 1,000 permutations and FDR 0.004 in both cohorts. All values in this paragraph are from the 1,000-permutation comparison runs; the primary values quoted above are from the 10,000-permutation run. It is therefore not true that every raised pathway loses significance.
- The 14-donor fit is unstable rather than simply weaker: 18 of 50 Hallmark sets, 532 of 1,257 Reactome sets and 992 of 2,305 GO:BP sets change the sign of their NES, and the number of significant sets moves in opposite directions between libraries (GO:BP 182 to 62, Reactome 127 to 249).
- Cell composition. The marker-based fractions give no clear evidence of a group difference: endocrine 3.91% in T2D against 2.98% in ND (Welch p = 0.42), acinar 4.50 against 3.74 (p = 0.66), ductal 0.26 against 0.35 (p = 0.41). These are non-significant results in 8 donors per group, so they do not establish that composition is equivalent, and an endocrine read fraction is a marker proxy rather than a count of beta cells. The endocrine fraction is numerically higher in T2D, but it cannot identify beta-cell abundance. INS itself is higher in T2D (log2FC +1.22, rank 1,830 of 17,358), as are PRSS1 (+1.24) and KRT19 (+1.28), so the reduced beta-cell set score comes from regulatory and secretory-machinery genes (PDX1, SLC2A2, ISL1, MAFB, STXBP1, ABCC8 and others in the leading edge) rather than from less hormone message. A composition contribution cannot be excluded on this evidence; deconvolution against a single-cell reference would be needed.
- Of the four genes Fadista et al. silenced, all four were tested here. TSPAN33 is lower in our T2D donors (log2FC -1.01, FDR 0.018). NT5E, TMED6 and PAK5 were not significantly differentially expressed (FDR 0.66, 0.85 and 0.84).

PART 3 CONNECTION
Part 3 reports 20 of 22 mitochondrial tRNAs with negative fold changes, consistent with the reduced mitochondrial RNA-processing enrichment. No promoter motif passes correction. These observations use the same cohort, so are complementary rather than independent evidence. Poly-A selection limits interpretation of small-RNA expression.

CAVEATS
- 8 donors per group. The gene-level analysis is underpowered, which is the reason this part uses a ranking of all genes rather than a gene list.
- Batch is partly confounded with group (6 of 8 T2D donors in batch A). Excluding P7_T2D and P8_T2D leaves batch B with no T2D donors, so the group effect in that fit rests on batch A alone (6 T2D against 3 ND). All three comparison pairs are from batch A. The same-size exclusions help distinguish sensitivity from sample size alone, but cannot separate individual donor effects from the changed batch representation.
- Preranked GSEA uses gene-label permutation, which ignores correlation between genes in the same pathway. The GSEA user guide states that with a small number of samples gene_set permutation gives a less stringent significance assessment and that a more stringent FDR cutoff such as 5% should then be used, which is the threshold we applied. Our 8 donors per group would meet the general sample-size recommendation for phenotype permutation, but the design includes batch and sex covariates, and phenotype permutation shuffles sample labels without preserving that structure, so it would test a null that ignores the batch confounding we specifically needed to adjust for. Ranking by the covariate-adjusted DESeq2 Wald statistic keeps the adjustment inside the ranking, at the cost of the less stringent permutation null. Both choices are stated here rather than one being presented as strictly better.
- The differential expression result is globally asymmetric: 56.1% of genes have a positive fold change, 90% of genes at raw p < 0.01 are higher in T2D, and the median Wald statistic is +0.17 rather than near zero. This asymmetry alone does not establish a technical or donor-specific cause.
- The reduced direction has no confirmation from a second method, because the 32 genes at raw p < 0.01 were too few for g:Profiler to return any term.
- 1,687 tested genes (8.8%) have no gene symbol and are excluded from GSEA; the libraries themselves cover only 68% (GO:BP) and 51% (Reactome) of the ranked genes.
- Enrichment measures coordinated change in transcript abundance. It is not a measurement of insulin secretion, mitochondrial activity, or the state of any individual cell, and bulk RNA-seq cannot distinguish fewer beta cells from beta cells expressing less. The marker-based composition check gives no clear evidence for the first explanation, but cannot exclude it.
- Our 16 donors are a subset of the 89 in Fadista et al., so agreement with that study is a re-analysis of their data and not independent replication.

CONCLUSION
In the full 16-donor cohort, T2D islets show a reduced beta-cell identity and secretion programme and reduced mitochondrial RNA processing, together with raised hypoxia, inflammation, extracellular matrix and glycolysis signatures. The donor-influence analysis helps distinguish their sensitivity. The hypoxia signature, and to a lesser extent the matrix and mitochondrial signals, are strongly influenced by P7_T2D and P8_T2D: removing those two donors, but not the three other T2D pairs we tested, lowers both the enrichment score and the underlying gene-level effect. That identifies sensitivity to removal of these donors and the associated change in batch representation. It does not identify the cause; islet isolation stress is a plausible explanation supported by Negi et al. 2012, not a demonstrated one. The beta-cell enrichment remains negative across all 21 refits, although its magnitude varies, and it retains significance in the comparison excluding P7/P8. Protein secretion reverses direction in that comparison and is not a stable finding. Inflammation-related sets also keep significance, so the raised direction is not uniformly donor-driven. The beta-cell result is the one we would defend; the hypoxia and matrix result should be reported as donor-dependent.
