Part 1: Data and pipeline

QUESTION
Are the reads of good quality, and where do they map in the genome?

DATA
Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) series GSE50244, Sequence Read Archive (SRA) study SRP029262, Fadista et al., PNAS 2014. Pancreatic islets from 89 deceased donors, Illumina HiSeq 2000, paired-end, 2 x 101 bases. GEO lists age, sex, body mass index (BMI) and glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), but not the diagnosis. We used the cut-offs of the original study: type 2 diabetes (T2D) with HbA1c >= 6.5% and non-diabetic (ND) with HbA1c < 6.0%. Donors with HbA1c 6.0-6.4% or without HbA1c were not used. This gave 11 T2D and 51 ND donors.
We chose 4 women and 4 men with T2D. Each was paired with an ND donor of the same sex and similar age and BMI (Table1_donors.csv).

METHODS
1. Download with fastq-dump --split-files (SRA Toolkit). All reads were used. The read numbers match the European Nucleotide Archive (ENA).
2. Quality check with FastQC.
3. Reference: human genome GRCh38 from Ensembl (HISAT2 index) and Ensembl gene annotation release 110 (GTF file).
4. Mapping with HISAT2, sorting and indexing with samtools. HISAT2 can split a read across an intron, Bowtie2 cannot.
5. Gene counts with StringTie -e and prepDE.py. HISAT2 and StringTie replace TopHat and Cufflinks in the new Tuxedo protocol.
6. Transcript assembly with StringTie and stringtie --merge (merged.gtf).
7. mapping_table.csv made with a Python script from the HISAT2 logs and the read names.

Software: SRA Toolkit 3.4.1, FastQC 0.13.0, HISAT2 2.2.3, SAMtools 1.25, StringTie 2.1.7.

STORAGE
All steps ran on one laptop (Windows Subsystem for Linux (WSL), 20 cores, 15 GB memory). The data take about 323 GB: FASTQ 248 GB, BAM 59 GB and reference 9.5 GB. They are kept on the laptop. This folder holds only the results.

RESULTS
- 382,369,864 read pairs, 14.8 to 32.0 million per donor.
- FastQC: all 32 files pass per sequence quality scores and adapter content. Per base sequence quality fails in both files of 9 donors. Per base sequence content fails in all files, which is typical for RNA-seq libraries.
- Mapping: 92.0% of the reads on average (86.2 to 96.7%). 81.6% of the read pairs map to one position (72.1 to 90.5%).
- 62,754 genes x 16 donors. 21,710 genes have no reads. Glucagon (GCG) is in the top 15 genes of every donor. Insulin (INS) ranges from 8,115 to 1,084,667 reads.
- merged.gtf: 284,533 transcripts, 31,934 of them not in Ensembl.

BATCH EFFECT
The read names show two flow cells.
  Batch A (C11YEACXX): 9 donors, 6 T2D and 3 ND, 89.6% mapped on average.
  Batch B (D088TACXX): 7 donors, 2 T2D and 5 ND, 95.1% mapped on average.
All batch A donors map lower than all batch B donors. The 9 donors that fail per base sequence quality are the batch A donors. Batch A holds 6 of the 8 T2D donors, so batch should be included in the DESeq2 model (design ~ batch + group).

CAVEATS
- T2D is defined by HbA1c only. Treated donors may be in the ND group.
- Two ND donors have HbA1c 5.7% (SRR957947) and 5.8% (SRR957909), in the prediabetes range of the American Diabetes Association (5.7-6.4%).
- Pair P1 differs in age (45 vs 62).
- In three ND donors (SRR957909, SRR957947, SRR957957) INS is not among the top 200 genes, which may reflect low islet purity.
- 8 vs 8 donors is a small sample.

CONCLUSION
The reads are of good quality. All 32 files pass the per sequence quality check and none needs adapter trimming. On average 92.0% of the reads map to the human genome (GRCh38), and 81.6% of the read pairs map to a single position, so the gene counts rest on reliable alignments. Quality and mapping are lower in batch A than in batch B, and batch A holds 6 of the 8 T2D donors. The data are suitable for the comparison of T2D and ND donors, provided that batch is included in the model.
